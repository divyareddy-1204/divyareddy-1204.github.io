When the AI gets it wrong

  • Quotes a line that isn't in the spec: confidence drops to zero and a person reviews it.
  • Invents a catalog ID: the line becomes unmapped.
  • Times out or returns junk: the app falls back to rules and shows a warning.
  • The AI never approves anything. It only decides what a person needs to look at.

A tracker that tells leads where to look

Checking the next one to two weeks of targets took most of a day. I built an Excel tracker that colours each job red for critical, orange for act now and green for on track. Leads work the red and orange jobs first.

Mapping the review chain

Electrical design feeds controls and mechanical review. Controls often starts early and checks against the design later. Handoffs broke because nobody could see current status or finish dates, so I built a shared status view and a recovery plan for late jobs.

Electrical design→ Controls review+ Mechanical review→ Release

Controls can start ahead of electrical and verify later, so status has to be visible to both.

One timesheet instead of a weekly roll-up

Engineers sent weekly reports and a supervisor copied them all into one sheet. A Power Automate form now writes every submission straight to that sheet.

Dashboards managers actually open
